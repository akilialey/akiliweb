Bid economics

What is this opportunity actually worth to you?

Contract value is the number on the notice. It isn't what the opportunity is worth. Before you commit a team, I work through four questions:

  1. What does it cost to chase?

    Your team's hours at their real cost, plus any outside help.

  2. What are your real odds?

    Based on the buyer, the incumbent, how it's scored, and whether you saw it coming.

  3. What's the lowest price that still pays?

    Delivery, overhead, compliance and payment terms — all in.

  4. Is it worth it?

    Your odds times the profit, minus what it costs to try — plus what it opens up next.

The goal isn't to bid on more. It's to put your people where the return is highest, and walk away from the rest with confidence.

Capture strategy

If you meet an opportunity when the RFP is published, you may already be behind.

The firms that win consistently tend to know the buyer, the history and the competition before the notice goes live. Capture is that groundwork. It can show you:

  • What departments and agencies buy, from whom, and for how much
  • Which contracts are coming up for renewal, and who holds them now
  • What similar contracts have been awarded for
  • Where your offer genuinely stands out against how the work will be scored
  • When buyers invite input — RFIs, industry days, consultations — and how to use it well

What capture can't do: it can't rig a fair competition, and once a solicitation is live, buyers limit contact with bidders. It doesn't guarantee a win. It makes sure you're not starting from zero.

The return

How this pays for itself — and what I won't promise.

I won't promise a win rate or a return. Nobody honest can. The value works through a few plain mechanisms:

  • Walk away from one bid you shouldn't chase, and you keep every hour it would have taken.
  • Spot a strong opportunity months earlier, and you compete prepared instead of rushed.
  • Price one major bid from real costs, and you protect your margin for the whole contract term.
  • Stop repeating the same scoring mistake, and every future bid starts stronger.

A serious bid can tie up weeks of senior time. Avoiding one that was never going to pay is often worth more than the engagement.
