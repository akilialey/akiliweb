Canadian small businesses are eligible for more grant funding than most people realize — the challenge is that it's scattered across four completely different systems, each with its own language and process.

## Federal grants

Programs run through agencies like ISED, NRC IRAP, or regional development agencies. These tend to be the largest in dollar value, but also the most competitive, with the longest applications and the strictest eligibility rules.

## Provincial grants

Ontario, for example, runs sector-specific and regional programs through various ministries. These are often less competitive than federal programs and better suited to smaller businesses — but they're also less publicized, so most owners never hear about them.

## Municipal grants

City-level programs (facade improvement, local hiring incentives, small business support funds) are usually the easiest to qualify for and the fastest to apply to — and the ones business owners are least likely to know exist.

## Private and foundation grants

Corporate and foundation-run grants often target specific groups — women-owned businesses, Indigenous-owned businesses, specific industries. These can be a strong fit if your business matches the funder's focus, but they require research to find in the first place.

**Where to start:** don't try to search all four systems yourself. Start with what you're trying to fund — equipment, hiring, expansion, R&D — and work backward from there. That's usually the fastest way to find a program you actually qualify for.
