If you've never responded to a government RFP before, the biggest shock usually isn't the writing — it's finding out how much of a proposal gets rejected before anyone even reads what you actually do.

Here are the five mistakes that quietly sink first-time bids.

## 1. Missing a mandatory requirement

Most RFPs list mandatory ("must-have") criteria separately from scored criteria. Miss one — a certification, an insurance minimum, a specific document format — and your bid can be disqualified before evaluation even starts. **No exceptions, no partial credit.**

## 2. Answering the work instead of the question

Evaluators score against a rubric, not a general impression of your business. If the RFP asks "describe your quality control process," and your answer describes your company history instead, you lose points — even if your quality control is excellent.

## 3. Underestimating the timeline

Between gathering documents, getting sign-off internally, and writing a response that actually addresses every criterion, most first-time bidders underestimate how long a strong submission takes. Rushed proposals read rushed.

## 4. Ignoring the scoring weight

Not every section is worth the same number of points. Spending equal time on a 5% section and a 30% section is a common — and costly — mistake.

## 5. Submitting with no internal review

A second set of eyes catches the gaps you can't see because you already know what you meant to say. Evaluators only know what's on the page.

**The good news:** none of this requires you to become a proposal expert. It requires someone on your side who already is.
