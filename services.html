01 · Bid economics · One opportunity · Fixed scope, fixed fee

Bid Economics Review

A defined assessment of one government opportunity, done before your team commits real resources to it. I look at the commercial economics, what the pursuit will take, how competitive you are, where the price needs to land, and the risks that matter. You get a clear go, go-with-conditions or walk-away, and the reasoning behind it.

Every opportunity goes through the Bid Economics Assessment: a structured look at its commercial, competitive, pursuit and strategic factors. The method is my own and isn't published. What you get is the answer and the reasoning.

Best for: a live RFP, tender or standing offer you're unsure about. Most useful when it's big, when there's a strong incumbent, or when margins look tight.

Timeline3–5 business days
ScopeOne opportunity
Walkthrough30-minute call
Send me the opportunity

What you get

  • A structured assessment of the opportunity's commercial, competitive, pursuit and strategic factors
  • The business case: an evidence-based estimate of your position, what the pursuit will cost, and what it's likely to return
  • The lowest price that still pays, and a range to aim for
  • The main win themes and the risks that could sink it
  • A clear recommendation, walked through on a 30-minute call
  • Major bids add: what similar contracts were awarded for, likely competitors, and risks in the contract terms

What I need from you

  • The solicitation, or a link to it
  • Your rough cost and rate information
  • Who would deliver the work

02 · Bid economics · Your whole bid history

Bid Desk Diagnostic

A hard look at your last two years of government bids: where your effort went, what it cost, what you won, and which wins actually made money. You leave with an assessment tuned to your own track record, so every future decision gets easier.

Best for: companies submitting 8 or more public bids a year — after a losing streak, before hiring a business development lead, or when government work feels busy but not profitable.

Timeline3–4 weeks
Looks back24 months
Readout90 minutes
Talk about a diagnostic

What you get

  • Win rate by buyer, bid type and contract size
  • What each bid cost you to pursue — and what it costs you to win $1 of government revenue
  • Margins on the contracts you won
  • Patterns in your wins, losses and evaluation scores
  • An assessment calibrated to your own track record
  • A written report and a prioritized 90-day action plan

What I need from you

  • A list of bids from the last 24 months, with outcomes (I can help build it)
  • Submitted prices, and any debriefs or scores
  • Rough hours spent per bid
  • Financial results for contracts you won

03 · Capture strategy · The next 12 months

Capture Plan

Stop meeting opportunities for the first time when the notice goes live. I map who buys what you sell, how much they spend, who holds the contracts now, and when those contracts come up — then turn it into a ranked list of pursuits and a 12-month calendar.

Best for: companies with a government growth target, a new offering, or a push into a new department, province, state or agency — including Canadian firms selling into the US and US firms selling into Canada.

Timeline4–6 weeks
Output12-month plan
RefreshQuarterly option
Talk about a capture plan

What you get

  • A market map built from public award data: buyers, spending, suppliers, contract values
  • Contracts expiring in your space, and who holds them
  • 10–20 target opportunities, ranked and timed
  • Positioning actions for each: who to know, what to prepare, when buyers invite input
  • A 12-month bid calendar

What I need from you

  • What you sell, and who you've sold it to
  • Past government contracts and bids
  • Your growth goals and capacity

04 · Bid strategy & proposal execution · After a “go”

Pursuit Lead

For bids that pass the test. I lead the response so the strategy survives the deadline: a compliance plan, a clear structure, win themes carried through every section, reviews, and a clean submission. Your subject-matter experts supply the substance; I make sure it scores.

Available after a Bid Economics Review. The review fee is credited toward Pursuit Lead if you go ahead. I don't take on bids that haven't been through the decision first — it's the whole point.

Fair questions

"Do you write proposals?"

I lead proposals for bids that have passed a Bid Economics Review. I don't sell proposal writing on its own — if you just need a writer, you'll get better value elsewhere.

"We already have a proposal team."

Good — they'll get better inputs. Most of my work happens upstream of them: what to bid, how to position, what to charge.

"Do you work with US companies?"

Yes. Most of my work is Canadian, and I also help Canadian firms selling into the US and US firms bidding in Canada. Canadian clients are billed in CAD; US clients in USD.

"Can you guarantee we'll win?"

No, and be wary of anyone who says they can. What I can do is make sure you're chasing the right bids, at the right price, as prepared as possible.

"Is my information safe?"

Everything you share stays between us. No subcontractors, no rotating team, and I never work with two clients on the same opportunity.
